# English — speak clearly at work

**Start:** run `npm run english` from the repo folder, then open today's journal.
For the full study session, run `npm run today`. English is an optional 15-minute block.

## Your starting point

- Intermediate English, as you described it. Tamil is your native language.
- Focus: professional conversations, grammar, natural vocabulary, pronunciation, compounds and idioms.
- Practise confidence, tact, listening and calm disagreement. Keep instructions and grammar explanations in English; use occasional Tamil translation pairs to clarify meaning.
- Begin with the first lesson below. A starting recording will show what needs attention; no level test or accent change is required.

## The 15-minute routine

1. **Listen · 3 min.** Follow the lesson's listening link. Replay a short passage and repeat its rhythm and pauses.
2. **Grammar · 3 min.** Make three sentences with the target grammar. Change the subject or time so you practise the choice.
3. **Speak · 5 min.** Record 60–90 seconds on your phone using the prompt. Listen back and choose one thing to improve.
4. **Retry · 4 min.** Fix that sentence or phrase, then speak again without reading. Keep 1–3 actual sentences under the journal's `## Notes`.

Tick the English lesson in the journal after using its grammar and two expressions from memory. If stuck, leave it unticked and try again next time. You do not need a perfect accent.
The command repeats an unticked lesson. Spoken reviews take priority over new lessons and return after 3, 7, 16, 35 and 75 days, then every 75 days.
Prepared lesson files and filing ticks do not count as speaking. Re-running either command keeps the day's existing session and your writing.

**Busy day:** say three sentences and repeat one corrected sentence. Leave the full-session tick until you complete its speaking task.

## Work with the assistant

- **Start:** "Practise English with me. Read General/english/README.md. Use today's lesson and ask one question at a time. Wait for my attempt."
- **Grammar:** "Check these sentences. Keep my originals. Explain the two most useful corrections in English. Occasionally compare Tamil translations of my sentence and the corrected version, then ask me to try again."
- **Pronunciation:** listen to a model and compare your recording. Audio is needed to assess pronunciation; typed sentences support grammar and wording feedback.
- **Role-play:** "Be a colleague asking for a deadline update. Let me answer before you suggest wording."
- **File:** write your own learning under `## Notes`, then say "file today's English notes". The study-notes skill keeps your wording in `## My recall` and puts researched guidance below.

Keep a short correction with **My sentence / Suggested version / Why / My retry**. Your first sentence and retry are yours to write; suggestions never become `My recall` automatically.
Tamil comparisons show a meaning difference when there is one. If both English versions translate alike, say so; do not invent an unnatural Tamil error to mirror an English one.
Choose a recurring error for the next lesson: articles, prepositions, subject–verb agreement, tense, question order or sentence linking. Add one lesson when an actual need appears.

## Film practice

Use a short scene you can replay. Record the film and scene; verify any quotation. Label a workplace adaptation or invented dialogue as original.
The starter film lesson links an official scene from *12 Angry Men*. Watching the whole film is optional. Discuss the reasoning, then practise a calm workplace version.

## Recordings and progress

Keep recordings on your phone; write the filename in your journal if useful. There is no upload or transcription step built into these commands.
Once a week, answer the same work-update prompt again. Compare grammar choices, useful expressions and whether your message is easy to follow. Save one sentence you improved.
English lessons stay in this folder. General's [index](../INDEX.md) is generated; each lesson's `My recall` starts empty until you supply your words.
