# Disagreeing and explaining trade-offs

**One line:** Acknowledge the other person's reasoning, explain a concern and propose a practical next step.

## Key points

- **Grammar:** for a real future possibility, practise `if + present simple, will + base verb`: "If we reduce the scope, we'll meet the deadline."
- **Three expressions:** **I take your point** acknowledges an argument; **I'm not so sure** signals doubt; **trade-off** names a compromise where one benefit costs another.
- **Original example:** "I take your point about speed. I'm not so sure we can keep every feature. The trade-off is scope versus delivery time."
- **Compound noun:** write **a trade-off**; use it with "between": "a trade-off between speed and flexibility".
- **Listen:** [Challenging someone's ideas](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/b2/challenging-someones-ideas). Compare how the speakers acknowledge an idea before questioning it.

## Say it aloud

**Original dialogue:** A: "Let's promise Friday and keep every feature." B: "I take your point about moving quickly. If we keep the full scope, we'll need more time."
**Prompt:** Your colleague wants a faster launch. Explain one trade-off and offer a conditional plan in 60–90 seconds. Keep the tone calm.
**Grammar switch:** propose the result of reducing scope, then the result of extending the deadline. Keep both conditions possible.
