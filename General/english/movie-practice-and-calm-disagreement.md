## Key points

- **Reference:** *12 Angry Men* (1957), directed by Sidney Lumet. Henry Fonda plays the dissenting juror. Use the official "A Scene From 12 ANGRY MEN" video on [Criterion's film page](https://www.criterion.com/films/27871-12-angry-men).
- **Grammar:** present continuous describes an action in progress: "He is questioning the assumption." Present simple expresses a general view: "A useful question tests an assumption."
- **Three speaking patterns — original:** "My concern is..." / "What supports that conclusion?" / "Let's consider another explanation."
- **Observation prompt:** choose a short exchange. Describe the question, pause or response you hear; assess whether you would use that tone at work.
- **Listening fallback:** if the scene will not play, use [Challenging someone's ideas](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/b2/challenging-someones-ideas). Listen for acknowledgment before disagreement.

## Say it aloud

**Original dialogue, not a film quote:** A: "The numbers are lower, so the launch failed." B: "My concern is that we're comparing different periods. Let's consider another explanation."
**Prompt:** Speak for 60–90 seconds: describe what a speaker is doing, state your view about good disagreement and give a workplace example.
**Grammar switch:** use "is questioning" for an action in the scene, then "questions" for a regular habit. Change the meaning deliberately.
