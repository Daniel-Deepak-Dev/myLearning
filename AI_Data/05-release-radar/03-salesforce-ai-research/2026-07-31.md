## AnchorBench's licence now covers the whole repository and bars use against three named competitors

On **July 30, 2026**, a single commit to `SalesforceAIResearch/AnchorBench` rewrote the README's licence section, replacing three lines with one. What went **out** was an explanatory note stating that **CC BY-NC 4.0 is a content licence and is not an OSI-approved open-source software licence** — a caveat that told readers, correctly, that this repository is not open source in the sense the term normally carries. What went **in** is a consolidated statement: *"The entire repository, including code, documentation, data, and results, is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International license (CC BY-NC 4.0). ... This dataset should not be used to compete with Anthropic, Google, or OpenAI."* Two separate changes are bundled in that one line, and they pull in opposite directions.

**The scope change is a tightening, and it is the part that affects you.** The earlier phrasing left room to read the CC BY-NC licence as attaching mainly to the data and results, with the runnable scorer and validation scripts perhaps sitting under something more permissive. The new sentence closes that reading: **code included**. *NonCommercial* in CC BY-NC 4.0 means you may not use the material primarily for commercial advantage or monetary compensation. Applied to the whole repository, that means **you cannot lift AnchorBench's scorer into a client engagement, a paid consulting deliverable, or an internal evaluation harness at a company that sells software** — not without separate permission. Reading the methodology and re-implementing the ideas yourself is unaffected; copying the artifact into commercial work is not.

**The competitor clause is the odd one, and worth reading precisely.** *"This dataset should not be used to compete with Anthropic, Google, or OpenAI"* names three companies that are **not Salesforce**, which is unusual enough to be worth pausing on. The most likely reading is that AnchorBench's evaluation pipeline uses those vendors' models as judges — the [July 29 note](2026-07-29.md) records Claude Sonnet 4.6 as the primary judge with GPT-4o and Gemini 2.5 Flash validating — and their API terms typically forbid using outputs to build competing models. A restriction like that would then be **inherited from the judge models' terms of service and passed downstream**, not an independent policy choice by Salesforce. That is an inference from the evidence, not something the commit states; the commit gives no rationale. Note also the wording: *"should not"*, and *"this dataset"* rather than *"this repository"* — softer and narrower than the licence sentence beside it, which is the kind of drafting that gets argued about later.

**What it means practically for you, and it generalises well past this one repo.** The durable lesson is that **an artifact produced by running other vendors' models can carry those vendors' restrictions forward into its own licence**, and you will typically discover this in a README rather than in a LICENSE file. Three habits follow. Check the licence on any benchmark or eval harness **at the commit you actually use**, because — as this commit demonstrates — licence text moves without any version number changing. Treat **CC BY-NC as a hard stop for client work** until someone with authority says otherwise, since "non-commercial" and "consulting deliverable" do not coexist. And when an eval pipeline uses third-party judge models, **assume the judges' terms travel with the output**, and check before you build anything commercial on top of it.

**Status:** README licence change, committed **July 30, 2026**. The repository is licensed **CC BY-NC 4.0 across code, documentation, data and results** — explicitly **not an OSI-approved open-source licence**, and now explicitly covering code. AnchorBench remains a public research artifact, **not a Salesforce product**, with no release train and no support commitment. The associated paper — *"Best Friends, Not Forever: Evaluating Long-Horizon Persona Collapse and Behavioral Drift in AI Companions"* — is **still not on arXiv or in any public index**, unchanged from the July 29 finding.

## What this scan did not find

**No new research output of any kind appeared between July 29 and July 31, 2026** — no paper, no model release, no benchmark, no Salesforce AI Research blog post, no press item. Across the organisation's repositories, only two moved in the extended window: **AnchorBench** on July 30 (the licence commit above) and **`agentforce-adlc`** on July 30, whose PR #47 is practitioner tooling rather than research and is written up in [today's Agentforce note](../01-agentforce/2026-07-31.md). Everything else sits outside the window — `gift-eval` last moved July 23, `oss-template` and `CRMArena` July 22, `SalesSim` July 18, `EVQA` June 27.

**The publish-in-bursts pattern continues to hold.** The [July 28 note](2026-07-28.md) proposed that this organisation publishes around conference cycles and goes quiet between them, and five scans have now been consistent with that: quiet since ICML in early July, one off-cycle repository release (AnchorBench, July 27) with no accompanying paper, and now a licence tidy-up on that same repository. **The next expected burst remains the NeurIPS 2026 cycle**, and a repository appearing before its paper does — as AnchorBench did — is the pattern worth watching for, because it is the earliest visible signal that a paper is coming.

**One claim carried forward, still unverified.** The AnchorBench paper remains unfindable. Searches across arXiv and general indexes for the exact title, and for the author names in combination with the subject matter, return only unrelated work on AI companion safety — [AICompanionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04867) and [Persona-Grounded Safety Evaluation of AI Companions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00227), neither of which is a Salesforce paper. The co-authors are verifiable Salesforce AI Research people and the repository is inside the official organisation, so the work is real; but **every headline number in the July 29 entry still comes from a README rather than a reviewed source**, and that should be said aloud before those figures are quoted to anyone.
