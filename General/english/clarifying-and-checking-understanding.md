# Clarifying and checking understanding

**One line:** Ask a precise question, then check that you understood the answer.

## My recall

<!-- Keep your own question and paraphrase after practice. -->

## Key points

- **Grammar:** inside an indirect question, keep statement word order: "Could you explain what the priority is?" Compare the direct question "What is the priority?"
- **Three expressions:** **clarify** = make the meaning clearer; **just to check** introduces a check; **on the same page** means sharing an understanding or agreeing about what to do.
- **Register:** "on the same page" is conversational. Plain alternative: "Let's confirm that we have the same understanding."
- **Original examples:** "Could you clarify which task comes first?" / "Just to check, do you need this today?" / "Are we on the same page about the scope?"
- **Listen:** [BBC indirect questions](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CzoxIVPtPgI). Repeat one question, then replace its topic with a work request.

## Say it aloud

**Original dialogue:** A: "Please make the update more useful." B: "Could you clarify what the audience needs? Just to check, is this for the project team?"
**Prompt:** Your colleague requests "a better report". Ask two precise questions, then paraphrase the request in 60–90 seconds.
**Grammar switch:** turn "When is the deadline?" into a question beginning "Could you tell me...". Say the full question aloud.
