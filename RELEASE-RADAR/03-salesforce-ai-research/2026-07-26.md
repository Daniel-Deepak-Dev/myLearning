**Who they are, in one paragraph.** Salesforce AI Research (`SFResearch`) is Salesforce's research organization — separate from the product teams that ship Agentforce and Data 360. They publish papers, open-source models and, increasingly, benchmarks that measure whether AI agents can really do enterprise work. They matter to a practitioner for a specific reason: they are the group publicly documenting where agents *fail* on CRM tasks, which makes their benchmarks the best available evidence for calibrating what you promise a client an agent will do.

## agentforce-adlc: the research org is now shipping practitioner tooling, not just papers

The most consequential thing Salesforce AI Research did in this window is not a paper — it is **`agentforce-adlc`** ("Agent Development Life Cycle"), a repo of **Claude Code skills** that build, deploy, test and optimize Agentforce agents via the **Agent Script DSL**, last updated **July 25, 2026** with daily commits from July 21–24. It packages four skills covering authoring plus a seven-category safety review (`/agentforce-generate`), preview and batch testing with adversarial probes (`/agentforce-test`), production session-trace analysis pulled from Data 360 (`/agentforce-observe`), and an OWASP LLM Top 10 security assessment (`/agentforce-secure`). In the same window the team merged **voice modality support** (July 23) and added **validation of generated agent assets against the AgentScript SDK** (July 24), so definitions are checked against the real grammar before they ever hit an org.

**Why this is the headline.** A research group publishing an opinionated lifecycle — requirements → author → safety-gate → deploy → adversarially test → analyze production traces → optimize — is a statement about what they think agent engineering *is*. It's also the most direct overlap between the Agentforce track and the Claude Code track anywhere in this repo: one artifact that is simultaneously a study lab for Agent Script and a serious worked example of Claude Code skill design. Check the licence before any client use — **CC BY-NC 4.0**, non-commercial, notably *not* the Apache-2.0 licence Agent Script itself carries.

**Status:** Open source, actively developed research tooling. No GA/Beta designation and no Salesforce support commitment. Requires Python 3.9+, Salesforce CLI v2.x, an Agentforce-enabled org.

**Sources:** [SalesforceAIResearch/agentforce-adlc](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/agentforce-adlc) · [Commit history](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/agentforce-adlc/commits/main) · [Salesforce AI Research on GitHub](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch)

## CRMArena-Pro is accepted to TMLR

**CRMArena-Pro** — Salesforce AI Research's benchmark for LLM agents on realistic CRM work — has been **accepted by TMLR** (*Transactions on Machine Learning Research*, a peer-reviewed ML journal), and its repo saw updates on **July 22, 2026**. The benchmark runs agents inside real Salesforce org environments built on Salesforce schemas, across both **B2B and B2C** variants, with multiple personas and interactive multi-turn scenarios; the datasets are on Hugging Face and the repo ships evaluation scripts so you can run your own model against it. The reported headline is uncomfortable and that is the point: agents do reasonably well on structured, single-turn workflow tasks — around **83%** — and degrade sharply on tasks needing sustained multi-turn context or judgement about what data they should not disclose, with confidentiality awareness close to absent unless the agent is explicitly prompted for it.

**What it means for you practically.** This is the citation to reach for when a stakeholder assumes an agent will simply "handle service cases." The failure profile is specific and actionable: structured lookups and updates are largely solved; *conversation state* and *data protection judgement* are not. In design terms that argues for narrow, well-scoped agents with deterministic Agent Script guardrails around anything touching sensitive fields, rather than one broad agent trusted to use discretion — which is exactly the architecture Multi-Agent Orchestration and the Einstein Trust Layer exist to support. Peer-reviewed acceptance matters here too: it makes the finding quotable in a design review rather than dismissible as vendor marketing.

**Status:** Accepted to TMLR (2026). Benchmark and datasets are open, explicitly **research-only** — not licensed for commercial deployment.

**Sources:** [SalesforceAIResearch/CRMArena](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/CRMArena) · [CRMArena-Pro paper (arXiv 2505.18878)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18878) · [Salesforce study warns against rushing LLMs into CRM workflows without guardrails (CIO)](https://www.cio.com/article/4008228/salesforce-study-warns-against-rushing-llms-into-crm-workflows-without-guardrails.html) · [Generative AI Benchmark for CRM](https://www.salesforceairesearch.com/crm-benchmark)

## GIFT-Eval keeps absorbing new time-series forecasting entrants

**GIFT-Eval**, Salesforce AI Research's open leaderboard for time-series forecasting models, took new result submissions on **July 23, 2026** (Metamorph 1.0, updated CastStar results) following entries on July 20 (LiteSpecFormer, LAIR base) and July 16 (RacineCast variants, HistRouter-CV, Falcon-Agent). This is routine leaderboard churn rather than a Salesforce product change, and most of the submissions come from outside teams — but the cadence is the signal: GIFT-Eval has become the venue where forecasting-model claims get standardized, and Salesforce owns the venue.

**Why an Agentforce practitioner should care at all.** Forecasting is the quiet other half of enterprise AI — pipeline, demand, capacity, churn timing — and it sits next to Salesforce's own `uni2ts` / Moirai foundation-model line. If a project ever asks "should we use a time-series foundation model or a classical method here?", GIFT-Eval is the neutral scoreboard to answer it from. Low urgency; worth a bookmark, not a study block.

**Status:** Ongoing open benchmark, Apache-2.0. No new Salesforce first-party model announced in this window.

## Background: 21 papers accepted to ICLR 2026

_Background, April 2026 — included to establish this folder's baseline._

Salesforce AI Research had **21 papers accepted to ICLR 2026** (the International Conference on Learning Representations, held April 23–27, 2026 in Rio de Janeiro), spanning LLM reasoning, evaluation systems, knowledge graphs and agent architectures. Salesforce frames the through-line as the problems it thinks matter for enterprise AI: agents that act reliably in complex environments, evaluations that expose real failure modes rather than flattering ones, stronger reasoning, and staying efficient and trustworthy at scale.

**Why it's here.** It tells you where product capability will come from in 12–24 months. The recurring theme across their portfolio — **evaluation frameworks that surface failure** — is the same instinct visible in CRMArena, SCUBA, GIFT-Eval and the adversarial testing baked into `agentforce-adlc`. That is a house style worth internalizing: this organization's default question about an agent is "how do we prove it's wrong?", and adopting that stance is most of what separates a production agent from a demo.

**Status:** Announced/published — ICLR 2026, April 2026.

## Background: SCUBA, the Salesforce Computer Use Benchmark

_Background, published 2025 — included to establish this folder's baseline._

**SCUBA** (Salesforce Computer Use Benchmark) evaluates *computer-use* agents — agents that drive a GUI by looking at the screen and clicking, rather than calling APIs — on CRM workflows in the Salesforce UI. It contains **300 task instances** derived from real user interviews across three personas: platform administrators, sales representatives and service agents. The results are sobering: in zero-shot settings, open-source-model agents score **under 5%** task success, closed-source-model agents up to **39%**; adding demonstrations lifts success to about **50%**.

**Why it matters practically.** It is the empirical answer to "why don't we just point a computer-use agent at Salesforce instead of building actions and MCP tools?" Because it fails roughly half to nineteen-twentieths of the time. That gap is precisely the argument for the Headless 360 / hosted-MCP direction: give the agent typed, permissioned, API-shaped access instead of pixels. The demonstration-augmented number is the other lesson — showing an agent a worked example is worth more than a bigger model.

**Status:** Published benchmark (2025), research-only.

**Sources:** [SCUBA: Salesforce Computer Use Benchmark (arXiv 2509.26506)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26506) · [Salesforce AI Research](https://www.salesforceairesearch.com/)
