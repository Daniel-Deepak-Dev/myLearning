# Giving clear work updates

**One line:** Explain what is done, what happened at a known time and what happens next.

## Key points

- **Grammar — result now:** present perfect uses `have/has + past participle`: "I've completed the review, so we can start testing."
- **Grammar — finished time:** use past simple with a finished time: "I completed the review yesterday." Avoid "I've completed it yesterday."
- **Three expressions:** **make progress** = move forward; **follow up** = take further action; **a follow-up** = the later action or message. The last is a compound noun.
- **Original examples:** "We've made progress on testing." / "I'll follow up tomorrow." / "I'll send a follow-up after the meeting."
- **Listen:** play the pronunciation on [follow up](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/follow-up); say it inside your own update and compare the two recordings.

## Say it aloud

**Original dialogue:** A: "What have you completed?" B: "I've reviewed the changes. I found an issue yesterday, and I'll follow up today."
**Prompt:** Give a 60–90 second update: one result now, one event yesterday and one next action. Use two target expressions.
**Grammar switch:** explain the same completed task once without a finished time and once with "yesterday". Check the verb form.
